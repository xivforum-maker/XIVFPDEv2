\abstractheading
  {Higher regularity for relaxed micromorphic models: from smooth to Lipschitz domains}
  {Sebastian Owczarek}
  {\affilWarsawTech}
  {Contributed talk}

The relaxed micromorphic model is a generalized continuum model designed to
describe the effective behaviour of microstructured materials and metamaterials.
It couples the classical displacement field with a non-symmetric microdistortion
tensor $P$ and, from the analytical point of view, combines an elasticity-type
system with a Maxwell-type system.

A characteristic feature of the model is that the curvature energy depends on
$\operatorname{Curl}P$ rather than on the full gradient $\nabla P$.
Consequently, weak solutions are naturally sought in the space
\[
H(\operatorname{Curl};\Omega)
=\left\{P\in L^{2}(\Omega):\operatorname{Curl}P\in L^{2}(\Omega)\right\},
\]
while the energy does not directly control all weak derivatives of $P$. This
naturally raises the question of whether one can recover control of the full
gradient $\nabla P$ from the structure of the system.

I will first briefly introduce the relaxed micromorphic model and recall earlier
existence and higher-regularity results, including local regularity and global
$H^{1}$-regularity of the microdistortion on smooth domains. The main part of
the talk will be devoted to a global regularity result for a physically nonlinear
version of the model on bounded Lipschitz domains. Since standard
difference-quotient arguments are not suitable in this setting, the proof is
based on modified finite differences constructed from inner variations together
with a Piola-type transformation adapted to the Curl structure. This yields, for
every $\varepsilon>0$,
\[
u\in H^{\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon}(\Omega),
\qquad
P,\operatorname{Curl}P\in H^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}(\Omega).
\]

The results presented in the talk are based on joint work with Dorothee Knees
and Patrizio Neff.
